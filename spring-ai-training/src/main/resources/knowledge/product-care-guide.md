# Product care and troubleshooting guide

This guide covers everyday care for the products we sell and the first steps to take when something goes wrong. Most problems
are solved by the steps below; if not, open a support ticket from your order page before sending anything back, because many
faults can be fixed remotely.

## Laptops

### Battery care

Lithium-ion batteries age fastest when they are kept at 100% charge and warm. If a laptop stays plugged in most of the day, turn
on the charge limit in the manufacturer's utility, which stops charging at 80%. Avoid leaving a laptop in a hot car or charging
it on a bed or sofa, where the vents are blocked.

A battery is considered worn when it holds less than 80% of its design capacity. The Latitude and Nomad laptops show this figure
under Settings > System > Battery health. Batteries are covered by the warranty for 1 year.

### A laptop that will not turn on

First connect the original charger and wait 10 minutes: a fully drained battery needs a few minutes before the power button
responds. If the charging light stays off, try another wall socket.

If the light is on but the screen stays black, hold the power button for 30 seconds to force a full shutdown, then press it once
again. A laptop that still shows nothing needs a support ticket; do not open the case, as that voids the warranty.

### Overheating and fan noise

Loud fans during video calls or games are normal. Constant fan noise when the laptop is idle usually means dust in the vents or a
background process. Clean the vents with short bursts of compressed air while the laptop is off, and check the task manager for a
process using the CPU.

The Latitude 16 Workstation throttles its discrete GPU above 87 degrees Celsius to protect it. Using it on a flat, hard surface
or a laptop stand keeps it below that limit in most workloads.

## Smartphones

### Screen and camera care

Clean the screen with a dry microfibre cloth; never use glass cleaner or alcohol wipes, which strip the oleophobic coating.
Foldables like the Aurora Fold 5 have a factory-fitted screen protector on the inner display that must not be removed, because
the display under it is soft.

Keep the camera lenses clean: most "blurry photo" complaints are caused by a fingerprint on the lens.

### Water resistance

The Pixelia 8 and 8 Pro are rated IP68: they survive 30 minutes at 1.5 metres in fresh water. Water resistance decreases over time
and is not covered by the warranty. After contact with salt water or chlorinated pool water, rinse the phone under tap water and
let it dry for 5 hours before charging. The Aurora Fold 5 is rated IPX4 only, which means splashes but not immersion.

### A phone that drains quickly

Battery drain in the first week after setup is normal while the phone indexes photos and restores apps. After that, check the
battery usage screen for an app using more than 20% in the background and restrict it. Turning off always-on display saves about
10% a day.

## Audio

### Pairing problems

If headphones or earbuds do not appear in the Bluetooth list, put them back in pairing mode: for the Studio 3 Over-Ear, hold the
power button for 7 seconds until the light flashes blue and white; for the AirBeat Pro Earbuds, keep them in the case with the lid
open and hold the case button for 5 seconds.

If they connect but the sound cuts out, forget the device on the phone, restart the phone and pair again. Microwave ovens and busy
2.4 GHz Wi-Fi can also interrupt Bluetooth audio.

### Active noise cancelling

Noise cancelling works best against steady sounds such as engines and air conditioning, and less well against voices. A whistling
sound when ANC is on usually means the ear cushions are not sealing: try a different ear tip size, or check that the cushions are
clipped in all the way round.

### The Boom 2 speaker and water

The Boom 2 Portable Speaker is rated IP67 and floats. Before charging it after it has been in water, close the USB-C port cover
and let it dry for at least 2 hours, or the charging will pause to protect the port.

## Monitors

### Dead and stuck pixels

A stuck pixel shows one colour all the time; a dead pixel is always black. A stuck pixel can sometimes be revived by running a
pixel-flashing video for 20 minutes. Our monitors are covered for dead pixels in the first 6 months under the warranty: 3 or more
dead pixels, or any dead pixel in the centre of the screen, qualify for a replacement.

### Image retention

IPS panels, like the whole ProView range, can show a faint ghost image after a static picture was displayed for hours. It is
temporary: showing a white screen or turning the monitor off for an hour clears it. Using a screen saver prevents it.

### USB-C connection

The ProView 27 QHD and 32 4K carry video, data and up to 65 W of charging over one USB-C cable. If the laptop charges but the
screen stays black, the cable is probably a charge-only cable: use the cable in the box, which supports DisplayPort Alt Mode.

## Storage

### Drive health

SSDs wear out slowly as data is written. The RapidNVMe drives are rated for 600 TB written per terabyte of capacity, far more
than most people write in the 5-year warranty. The free Rapid Toolbox utility shows the percentage of rated life used.

Keep at least 10% of an SSD free: a full drive becomes noticeably slower when writing.

### Memory cards

Format a new memory card in the camera that will use it, not on a computer. If a camera reports a card error, copy the files off
first, then format the card; repeated errors mean the card should be replaced under the warranty.

## Accessories

### Keyboards and mice

The Compact Mechanical Keyboard has hot-swappable switches: a key that registers twice can be fixed by pulling the switch with the
included tool and pressing it back in. The Precision Wireless Mouse has a battery life of about 70 days; when the light blinks
red, charge it for 5 minutes for a full day of use.

### Docks and chargers

The USB-C 11-in-1 Dock needs a charger of at least 100 W to pass 100 W through to the laptop; with a smaller charger the laptop
may charge slowly or not at all. The GaN 100 W Charger shares its power between ports: with two devices connected, the first
USB-C port gets at most 65 W.
